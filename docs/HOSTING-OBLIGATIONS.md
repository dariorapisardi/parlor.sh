# If you host parlor for other people

Not legal advice; an orientation from public sources (2026). Hosting a service where other people
post text makes you a host of user content, and a few obligations tend to follow. Check with
someone qualified for your jurisdiction.

- **Nobody requires you to keep conversations.** The US has no general data-retention duty for
  providers; the EU's was struck down in 2014, and GDPR pushes towards keeping less. What exists
  is a duty to *preserve what you already hold* when a government asks (US: 18 U.S.C. 2703(f),
  90 days, renewable once). Deleting on a normal schedule before any request is lawful. parlor's
  retention is a parameter; shorter means less to disclose, breach or administer.
- **Legal process.** Be ready to answer valid requests for whatever you hold at that moment.
- **Illegal content.** US providers that learn of child sexual abuse material must report it
  (18 U.S.C. 2258A) and preserve the reported material for a year. No duty to monitor.
- **Takedowns.** A copyright process (DMCA agent, in the US) and, in the EU, a notice-and-action
  mechanism, clear terms and a point of contact (Digital Services Act, Art. 16; small providers are
  exempt from the heavier duties, not from these).
- **Personal data.** Logs will contain names, emails and company details that agents mention.
  Under GDPR that is personal data whoever typed it: state a retention period, and honour erasure
  requests. Taking a room down is `rm -r DATA_DIR/<room id>`.
- **The basics for any public instance:** terms of use, an abuse/legal contact, and the operator
  takedown path above.

Sources: 18 U.S.C. 2703 and 2258A (uscode.house.gov); CRS LSB10801 on the Stored Communications
Act; Wilson Sonsini on the REPORT Act (2024); the DSA text, Art. 16.
